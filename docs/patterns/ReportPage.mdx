# Report page

A single-column, long-form frame for situation reports, flash updates, and analytical pieces — header, a hero or page title, a byline and date, then an article body capped at a readable measure, above the footer.

## When to use

- The content is **narrative**: a reader follows it top to bottom rather than scanning a grid.
- You have **authored, dated** material — a sitrep, a flash update, a press piece, a thematic brief.
- The body mixes **prose, section headings, figures with captions, and bullet lists**, and benefits from a fixed reading measure.
- A **table of contents** would help the reader jump between long sections.
- **Use something else when** the goal is to monitor many metrics side by side — reach for the [Dashboard shell](?path=/docs/patterns-dashboard-shell--docs) instead.

## Structure

```html
<header class="cd-header">
  <!-- cd-global-header + cd-site-header (see Composites/Header) -->
</header>

<main>
  <!-- Feature reports: hero. Routine sitreps: just the page title. -->
  <section class="cd-hero">
    <div class="cd-hero__container">
      <h1 class="cd-hero__title">Sudan situation report</h1>
      <p class="cd-hero__description">Humanitarian needs and response, September 2026.</p>
    </div>
  </section>

  <article class="cd-article">

    <!-- Byline + date -->
    <div class="cd-byline">
      <div class="cd-byline__wrapper">
        <div class="cd-byline__image cd-byline__image--rounded">
          <img src="author.jpg" alt="Author name">
        </div>
        <div class="cd-byline__content">
          <div class="cd-byline__name">OCHA Sudan</div>
          <div class="cd-byline__text">Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs</div>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
    <div class="cd-date">
      <div class="cd-date__wrapper"><span class="cd-date__day">20</span></div>
      <div class="cd-date__wrapper">
        <span class="cd-date__month">Sep</span>
        <span class="cd-date__year">2026</span>
      </div>
    </div>

    <!-- Optional table of contents -->
    <nav class="cd-toc" aria-label="Table of contents">
      <h2 class="cd-toc__title">Contents</h2>
      <ol class="cd-toc__list">
        <li><a href="#overview">Overview</a></li>
        <li><a href="#needs">Humanitarian needs</a></li>
        <li><a href="#response">Response</a></li>
      </ol>
    </nav>

    <!-- Body: prose, section headings, figures, bullets -->
    <p>An introduction paragraph sets the context for the report.</p>

    <h2 class="cd-block-title cd-block-title--underline" id="needs">Humanitarian needs</h2>
    <p>Section prose, constrained to a readable measure by cd-article.</p>

    <ul class="cd-figure-list">
      <li class="cd-figure-list__item">
        <div class="cd-figure-list__label">People in need</div>
        <div class="cd-figure-list__value">18.4 million</div>
      </li>
    </ul>

    <figure>
      <img src="chart.svg" alt="Funding by sector, 2026">
      <figcaption class="cd-caption">Source: OCHA, FTS, 2026.</figcaption>
    </figure>

    <ul class="cd-bullet-list">
      <li>Key point one.</li>
      <li>Key point two.</li>
    </ul>

    <a href="#" class="cd-read-more">Read the full report</a>

  </article>
</main>

<footer class="cd-footer">
  <!-- see Composites/Footer -->
</footer>
```

## Responsive

This pattern is single-column at every width, so the work is keeping the reading measure honest — values from `tokens/brand.css`.

- **Below `--cd-bp--sm` (576px)** — full-bleed body with `--cd-container-padding` (12px) gutters. The `cd-byline` and `cd-date` stack; the `cd-toc` runs as a single column.
- **`--cd-bp--md` (768px)** — gutters widen to `--cd-container-padding-tablet` (24px). `cd-article` paragraphs hold at `--cd-max-content-width` (820px) and stay left-aligned even as the viewport grows.
- **`--cd-bp--lg` (1024px) and up** — the column stays centred at 820px while the header and footer span the full `--cd-max-width` (1220px). On `--cd-bp--xl` (1200px+) a long `cd-toc` may switch to `cd-toc--with-cols`. Never let body text exceed 820px — long line lengths hurt readability.
